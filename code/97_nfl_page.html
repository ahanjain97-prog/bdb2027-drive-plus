Drive+

How fast a corner gets going again after he opens his hips. It comes from one combine drill, and on Sundays it shows up in one job: turning and carrying a receiver in zone. Corners who grade high stay tighter after the receiver's break and give up fewer catches. In man coverage it tells you little.

What the number is

One drill, one moment: his top speed after the turn

The drill is the combine's backpedal-and-transition. The DB pedals about 15 yards, opens his hips on the coach's cue, turns roughly 40 degrees and sprints away on the new line. No ball, nothing to read: a pure turn-and-run. Drive+ ignores the pedal and the turn itself and grades only how fast he gets going afterwards.

Reading the number

100 is an average corner. Every 10 points shows up in catches allowed.

Drive+ reads like a plus stat: 100 is the average 2023–25 corner, and 10 points is one standard deviation. Below the scale, three readouts line up with it: roughly how fast that corner is moving after the turn, how often his man catches the ball when targeted, and how often his man is open at the throw.

    What we can't explain yet: catches lost after the throw

    Completion % allowed when targeted, a 110 vs a 90 Drive+ DB on the registered 3-yd reps, split on the same throws into what separation, depth and speed at the release predict and what happens after the ball is in the air.     For the staff

    How a staff would use it

    Fit

    Grade the snap, not the scheme label

    It predicts the reps where a corner turns and carries a receiver in zone. A team's overall Cover 3/4/6 share did not sharpen it, so weigh it by how often you will ask him to match and carry, not by the coverage name on the call sheet.

    Evaluate

    Watch steps three to five

    The corner who looks explosive for two steps and then tops out is not the one you want carrying a post. His speed coming out of the turn says little about Sundays; the link builds through the drive and is strongest 0.8–1.0 s after the turn.

    Measure

    Get two more reps

    One attempt is a noisy read; two (the combine norm) are better; four are close to a settled grade. Two extra reps at a pro day are cheap, and the clip has to run 1.5 seconds past the turn, which the 2024 combine clips did not always do.

    Expect

    Quarterbacks will adjust

    QBs keep throwing to his side but go deeper. The catches still drop, but his side's EPA doesn't move and breakups don't rise, so pair Drive+ with ball skills from film.

    How sure are we

    It holds up 47 of 48 ways we cut it

    With about 50 corners and 500 targets, we wrote the bar down before looking: right direction, unlikely to be luck, and an 80% interval that stays on one side of zero. Six claims, eight ways of slicing the data. The one miss is completions in the 2024 season alone: right direction, too few targets to be sure.
