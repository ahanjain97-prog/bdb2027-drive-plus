NFL Big Data Bowl 2027 · combine tracking → NFL coverage · 2023–25

Drive+

One combine drill, one number: how fast a defensive back is moving one second after he turns out of his backpedal. On Sundays it is a zone-match grade: when a corner is matching a receiver in zone on a route that keeps going, it predicts how tight he stays and how often the ball is caught. In man coverage it predicts little.

The combine · backpedal-and-transition (45°) drill

It isn't the first step. It's steps three to five.

Every DB backpedals, breaks on the coach's cue and drives. The first two steps out of the turn barely separate anyone. What separates corners is the speed they reach 0.8–1.0 seconds after the turn, their top speed in the drive. Pick any two corners and press Race out of the break.

  1. Smooth the 10 Hz combine positions into speed and running direction.
  2. Find the backpedal line, then the turn: direction swings 25–90° off it at 3+ yd/s.
  3. From the end of the turn, take his top speed in the drive (up to 2 s). Average his attempts.
  4. Scale within his combine class, then to the 2023–25 corners: 100 = average, 10 = 1 SD.

    Higher grade, tighter coverage, fewer catches

    The line is the evidence: a fit across every rep with a 90% band from resampling players. Dots are each DB's own average and are noisy at small samples. The middle panel is the composite coverage score, the registered criterion: four context-adjusted measures averaged per rep, which is more reliable than any one of them (player-mean reliability .70). Across players, Drive+ correlates .29 with the composite as measured and about .42 once the noise in both is accounted for.

    The throw · 2023–25 zone coverage

    QBs don't avoid high-Drive+ corners. They throw deeper, and it doesn't work.

    Every zone pass with a graded corner on a clear side of the field. Each zone on his side is shaded by how its completion rate compares with the same depth on the other side: red means more completions at him, blue fewer. Against bottom-third corners, deep balls to their side land 11 points more often. Against top-third corners, the gap disappears.

    Outside-data check: nflverse throw difficulty and NGS receiver quality

    Not part of the registered tests. nflverse play-by-play joins to these plays exactly (completion flags agree on 100% of matched throws) and supplies a completion probability for every throw, including throws to receivers outside the tracked pool; NGS receiving aggregates supply each targeted receiver's season separation.

    A separate hypothesis: what happens after the throw

    Completion % allowed when targeted, a 110 vs a 90 Drive+ DB on the registered 3-yd reps, split on the same throws into what separation, depth and speed at the release predict and what happens after the ball is in the air. The after-throw share has no measured mechanism and is concentrated in 2025, so it is registered on its own; the movement claims above are judged without it.
